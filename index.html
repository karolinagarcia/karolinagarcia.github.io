About Me

I'm an astrophysicist and CAPS Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. I earned my PhD in Astronomy from the University of Florida, after transitioning from a career in engineering and industry.

My research focuses on galaxy formation and evolution, connecting the physics of the interstellar medium to cosmological observations. I combine simulations, radiative transfer, and machine learning to model atomic and molecular line emission across cosmic time. I'm also the lead developer of SLICK.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about science communication and education. Outside astronomy, I enjoy singing, playing guitar, and sports.

Research

Research vision

Connecting the physics of galaxies to the observable Universe

My research focuses on understanding how the physical processes governing galaxy formation shape the atomic and molecular emission we observe across cosmic time. I develop computational approaches that connect the small-scale physics of the interstellar medium to observable signatures in galaxies and across cosmological volumes.

At the center of this effort is SLICK, a framework I developed to predict line emission from cosmological simulations. My long-term goal is to use this approach to investigate galaxy evolution, black hole activity, and the astrophysical uncertainties that shape future cosmological measurements.

Core research program

Lead developer

SLICK

From gas physics to emission-line observables

SLICK predicts molecular and atomic emission from the physical conditions of gas in cosmological simulations, rather than relying solely on empirical galaxy-wide luminosity relations. Combining gas modeling with radiative-transfer calculations, it allows us to investigate not only how galaxies emit light, but why.
