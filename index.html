About Me

I'm an astrophysicist and CAPS Survey Science Fellow at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. I earned my PhD in Astronomy from the University of Florida, after transitioning from a career in engineering and industry.

My research focuses on galaxy formation and evolution, connecting the physics of the interstellar medium to cosmological observations. I combine simulations, radiative transfer, and machine learning to model atomic and molecular line emission across cosmic time. I'm also the lead developer of SLICK.

Beyond research, I'm passionate about science communication and education. Outside astronomy, I enjoy singing, playing guitar, and sports.

Research Projects

SLICK: Modeling the Interstellar Medium

I develop SLICK, a framework for predicting molecular and atomic line emission from cosmological hydrodynamical simulations. By combining models of interstellar medium physics, radiative transfer, and machine learning, SLICK connects the small-scale physical conditions of galaxies to their observable emission.

Line-Intensity Mapping

I investigate how emission from galaxies contributes to line-intensity mapping signals across cosmic time. My work includes predictions for CO and [CII] luminosity functions, line ratios, and power spectra, as well as collaborations with observational line-intensity mapping experiments.

AGN and Molecular Gas

I study how radiation from active galactic nuclei influences the physical and chemical conditions of the interstellar medium, particularly the emission of molecular and atomic lines.

Galaxy Clusters in the Boötes Field

I lead the development and characterization of a galaxy cluster catalog in the Boötes field, identifying 825 cluster candidates across a broad range of redshifts. This work combines multiwavelength observations, photometric redshifts, and cluster detection methods.

Cosmological Statistics and Supernovae

My previous research includes methods for measuring the anisotropic three-point correlation function of galaxies and forecasting cosmological constraints from Type Ia supernova peculiar velocities.
